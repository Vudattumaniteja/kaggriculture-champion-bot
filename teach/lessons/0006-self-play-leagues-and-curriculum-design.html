Self-Play Regimes, League Architecture & Accelerated Curriculum

How much self-play to use, how to avoid Rock-Paper-Scissors cycles, and how to train 10x faster with flight simulator jumpstarts.

Core Learning Objective: Discover the optimal sparring ratio for competitive AI training (The 40/40/20 Rule), how Prioritized Fictitious Self-Play (PFSP) stops the AI from forgetting past strategies, and how the 80/10/10 Fast-Forward Curriculum focuses compute on critical moments.

Part 1: The Non-Technical Guide (Intuition First)

1. The "Championship Sparring Camp" (The 40 / 40 / 20 Rule)

Imagine an Olympic champion boxer preparing for a world title fight. How should they spend their sparring rounds?

  • 40% Sparring with an Identical Twin (Active Mirror Self-Play):
    You spar against someone who copies your current speed, technique, and style. When you make a small defensive mistake, they exploit it immediately. This pushes your limits.
  • 40% Rematches Against Past Tough Rivals (PFSP Historical Pool):
    You don't waste time sparring partners you beat 100% of the time. You specifically review tape and spar against past opponents who knocked you down or exposed your weaknesses. This ensures you never forget how to beat older strategies.
  • 20% Sparring with Extreme Style Specialists (Fixed Heuristics):
    You invite an aggressive wrestler, a tall southpaw kickboxer, and a rapid counter-puncher into camp. Even if their style is one-dimensional, it ensures you never forget how to handle a sudden rush or an unorthodox attack.

In Kaggriculture: If our bot only plays against itself, it can get stuck in a "Rock-Paper-Scissors" loop (Strategy A beats B, B beats C, C beats A). By using the 40% Active Self-Play + 40% Past Rivals + 20% Heuristic Specialists mix, our bot develops an invincible, well-rounded game.

2. The "Flight Simulator" Analogy (The 80 / 10 / 10 Curriculum)

Airline pilots do not spend 90% of their flight simulator training flying in a straight line at 35,000 feet on autopilot. That would waste time and money.

How Flight Simulators Train Pilots:

  • 80% Full Flights: Normal takeoff to landing to maintain procedure.
  • 10% Mid-Flight Engine Failure (Midgame Jumpstart): The pilot is instantly placed at 30,000 feet with an engine on fire to drill emergency handling.
  • 10% Blizzard Landing (Endgame Jumpstart): The pilot is placed 2 miles from the runway in heavy crosswinds to drill precision touchdown.

In Kaggriculture: Days 1 to 10 are simple tilling and basic watering. The hardest decisions happen on Day 14 (capital expansion) and Day 26 (final crop harvest & market liquidation).

Our curriculum fast-forwards 20% of training games directly to Days 14 and 26 in less than 150 milliseconds on the CPU, letting the AI practice critical moments 10 times more often.

3. Cooling Down Exploration (The Gumbel Temperature)

When a teenager learns chess, they try bizarre moves to see what happens (high curiosity/temperature). When a grandmaster plays in the world championship, they play the sharpest, most calculated move every turn (zero temperature).

We use a Two-Stage Temperature Schedule ($\tau = 1.0 \to 0.0$):

  • Early Training: High temperature ($\tau = 1.0$) + Dirichlet exploration noise to discover bold new crop combinations and early land expansion timings.
  • Late Training / Tournament Play: Zero temperature ($\tau = 0.0$) for razor-sharp, deterministic execution.

Part 2: Technical Blueprint & Mathematics

1. Prioritized Fictitious Self-Play (PFSP) Formulation

Let $\mathcal{H} = \{\pi_1, \pi_2, \dots, \pi_N\}$ be the pool of historical checkpoints. The sampling probability $P(i)$ is weighted by the Champion's loss rate against that checkpoint: 

P(i) = max( 0.05, ( 1.0 - WinRate_i )^1.5 ) / Sum_Weights

Checkpoints that defeated the Champion recently receive 20x higher training frequency, forcing the policy to patch its tactical vulnerabilities immediately.

2. Isolated Sub-Trajectory GAE (No Warmup Contamination)

When fast-forwarding to Day 14 ($t_{\text{start}} = 336$), we compute Generalized Advantage Estimation strictly on the neural portion of the trajectory ($t \ge t_{\text{start}}$): 

delta_t = r_t + gamma * V(s_{t+1}) - V(s_t)   # for t >= t_start
A_t = sum_{l=0}^{T - t - 1} (gamma * lambda)^l * delta_{t+l}
# Gradients for t < t_start are strictly 0.0 (Zero heuristic bias)

3. Mask-Safe Dirichlet Exploration Noise

To add exploration noise without violating legal action rules, Dirichlet noise $\boldsymbol{\eta} \sim \text{Dir}(\alpha)$ is blended strictly over the valid action mask: 

pi'(a_k) = (1.0 - eps) * pi(a_k) + eps * eta_k   # only where mask[k] == 1

4. 500k Binary SumTree Prioritized Replay Buffer (PER)

Transitions are stored in an $\mathcal{O}(\log N)$ Binary SumTree where sampling priority is proportional to Temporal Difference error: $p_i = (|\delta_i| + \epsilon)^{0.6}$.

Summary of League & Curriculum Architecture

  1. The 40/40/20 Sparring Rule: 40% Active Self-Play Mirror + 40% PFSP Past Rivals + 20% Fixed Heuristic Specialists.
  2. The 80/10/10 Jumpstart Curriculum: Fast-forward 20% of games to Midgame (Day 14) and Endgame (Day 26) to drill high-leverage decisions.
  3. Mask-Safe Dirichlet & Gumbel Cooling: Maintain legal exploration early and lock in deterministic grandmaster play late.
